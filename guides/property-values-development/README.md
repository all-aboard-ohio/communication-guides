# Communication Guide: How to Discuss Property Values & Development

**Subtopic:** TOD (Transit-Oriented Development) data

## Overview

This guide provides data-driven talking points and resources for communicating the economic development benefits of passenger rail investment. It frames rail not as a transit expense but as a community development catalyst, with a focus on Transit-Oriented Development (TOD) outcomes that resonate with planners, developers, and residents.

## Goals

- Communicate the economic development benefits of passenger rail investment clearly
- Frame rail not as a transit expense but as a community development catalyst
- Provide data-driven talking points on TOD outcomes that resonate with planners, developers, and residents

## Key Talking Points

### TOD Density Metrics

- Rail stations enable higher-value land use by concentrating development around transit nodes
- Typical TOD projects achieve 2–4 times the density of conventional suburban development
- Mixed-use zoning near stations increases land value by 10–30% compared to single-use zones
- Density bonuses and reduced parking requirements can increase developable area by 20–50%

### Property Value Appreciation

- Documented increases near rail stations pre- and post-opening:
  - **Pre-opening:** Properties within 1/2 mile of planned stations appreciate 5–15% faster than regional averages
  - **Post-opening:** Values typically stabilize 10–20% higher than comparable non-rail areas within 3–5 years
- Premium for walkable, transit-accessible locations: $50–$100 per square foot in urban markets
- Commercial properties near stations see 15–30% higher lease rates

### Retail and Commercial Activation

- Walkable station areas generate 20–40% more tax base per acre than auto-oriented development
- Retail vacancy rates near rail stations are 30–50% lower than regional averages
- Each rail station supports 50–200 new retail/service jobs within a 10-minute walk shed
- Restaurant and entertainment venues see 25–50% higher revenue in station areas

### Housing Access

- Rail expands affordable housing options by reducing car dependency costs (savings of $5,000–$10,000 per household per year)
- TOD projects typically include 10–20% affordable housing units when required by zoning
- Reduced parking requirements lower construction costs by 15–25%, enabling more units at lower price points
- Transit proximity increases housing choice for low- and moderate-income households

## Example Use Case

An advocate is presenting to a neighborhood association concerned about "what rail will do to our area." Using this guide, they show documented property value trends, highlight community-controlled TOD success stories, and reframe rail as a tool for community wealth-building rather than displacement.

**Sample Talking Points for This Scenario:**

1. "Studies show that property values near rail stations increase 10–20% over 3–5 years, but this growth is manageable with community benefit agreements."
2. "TOD doesn't have to mean displacement — cities like Portland and Denver have used community land trusts and inclusionary zoning to keep housing affordable."
3. "Rail reduces household transportation costs by $5,000–$10,000 per year, effectively increasing disposable income for existing residents."
4. "Local businesses near stations see 20–40% more foot traffic and higher sales — that means more jobs and tax revenue for our community."
