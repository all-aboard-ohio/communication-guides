# How to Use Data Visualization in Advocacy

**Subtopic:** Maps, charts, interactive dashboards

## Overview

Data visualization is one of the most powerful tools in an advocate’s communication toolkit. A well-designed chart or map can convey complex information at a glance, making your argument more compelling and shareable than text alone. This guide will help you select the right visualization type, design it for accessibility and impact, and distribute it effectively across social media and earned media channels.

## Goals

- Show advocates how effective visualization amplifies data impact beyond what text alone can achieve
- Provide practical guidance on tool selection, design best practices, and distribution strategy
- Help advocates create graphics that drive social media engagement and earn media coverage

## Key Talking Points

### 1. Chart Type Selection: Matching the Right Visualization to the Data Story

Choosing the wrong chart type can confuse your audience or misrepresent your data. Here’s a quick reference:

| Data Story | Recommended Chart Type | Example Use Case |
|---|---|---|
| Comparing values across categories | Bar chart | State-by-state transit funding per capita |
| Showing trends over time | Line chart | Annual ridership growth over 10 years |
| Part-to-whole relationships | Stacked bar or pie chart (use sparingly) | Mode share: car vs. transit vs. bike |
| Geographic distribution | Map (choropleth or dot density) | Transit deserts in Ohio counties |
| Correlation between two variables | Scatter plot | Population density vs. transit use |
| Ranking | Horizontal bar chart | Top 10 cities by transit investment |

**Rule of thumb:** If your audience can’t understand the chart in 5 seconds, simplify it.

### 2. Color and Accessibility Best Practices

Your visualizations must work for everyone, including people with color vision deficiencies (CVD) and those using screen readers.

- **Use colorblind-friendly palettes:** Avoid red-green combinations. Use tools like [ColorBrewer](https://colorbrewer2.org/) or [Viz Palette](https://projects.susielu.com/viz-palette) to select accessible palettes.
- **Add patterns or textures:** For bar charts and maps, use hatching or dot patterns in addition to color.
- **Include direct labels:** Place data labels directly on or near data points instead of relying solely on a legend.
- **Provide alt text:** Write descriptive alt text for every visualization (e.g., “Bar chart showing Ohio highway spending of $50 billion vs. rail spending of $2 billion from 1990 to 2020”).
- **High contrast:** Ensure text and data elements have sufficient contrast against the background (WCAG AA minimum).

### 3. Interactive vs. Static: When Each Format Is Most Effective

| Format | Best For | Channels |
|---|---|---|
| **Static** (image) | Quick social media posts, press releases, one-pagers | Twitter/X, Facebook, Instagram, print |
| **Interactive** (web-based) | Deep dives, data exploration, embedding in blog posts | Websites, newsletters, advocacy portals |

- Use **static** when you want a single, clear message that can be shared instantly.
- Use **interactive** when your audience needs to explore data themselves (e.g., filtering by county or year).
- **Hybrid approach:** Create a static image for social media and link to an interactive version on your website.

### 4. Mobile-Friendly Design

Most audiences will view your visualization on a phone. Design accordingly:

- **Optimize for small screens:** Use a vertical layout, large fonts (at least 14px), and minimal clutter.
- **Test on mobile:** Preview your graphic on a phone screen before publishing.
- **Avoid tiny details:** Remove gridlines, reduce axis labels, and simplify legends.
- **Use responsive tools:** Tools like Datawrapper and Flourish automatically adjust charts for mobile.

## Example Use Case

An advocate wants to make the case on social media that Ohio’s highway spending dwarfs rail investment over the past 30 years. Using this guide, they:

1. **Select a chart type:** A horizontal bar chart comparing total highway vs. rail spending (1990–2020).
2. **Apply accessible colors:** Use a blue/orange palette (colorblind-friendly) with direct data labels.
3. **Export a mobile-optimized graphic:** Vertical layout, large font, no legend.
4. **Pair with a caption framework:**
   - **Hook:** “Ohio spent 25x more on highways than rail over the last 30 years. 🚗🚂”
   - **Data:** “$50 billion on highways vs. $2 billion on rail (1990–2020).”
   - **Call to action:** “Tell your state rep to invest in rail. Link in bio.”

### Accessibility Checklist

Before publishing, verify:

- [ ] Color palette is colorblind-friendly (test with [Coblis](https://www.color-blindness.com/coblis-color-blindness-simulator/))
- [ ] Alt text is written and descriptive
- [ ] Text size is at least 14px on mobile
- [ ] No reliance on color alone to convey information
- [ ] High contrast between text and background
- [ ] Interactive elements are keyboard-navigable (if applicable)
